NHS pay 2027/28: what the 2.4% plan means for you

On 19 October the government told the NHS pay review bodies it is planning for a 2.4% pay rise in England next year. Here is what that covers, what happens next and where things stand in each UK nation.

What happened

The Department of Health and Social Care and NHS England sent their evidence for the 2027/28 pay round to two independent review bodies: one for Agenda for Change staff (nurses, midwives, health visitors, healthcare assistants, allied health professionals and many more) and one for doctors and dentists, including GPs.

Both say the department’s plans “currently plan for a pay uplift of 2.4%”, and that anything higher would mean trade-offs elsewhere in the health budget. This applies to England.

Agenda for Change evidence on GOV.UK →
Doctors and dentists evidence on GOV.UK →

What 2.4% looks like

On a salary of £30,000, a 2.4% rise is £720 a year before tax and deductions, or about £60 a month. On £50,000 it is £1,200 a year, or about £100 a month.

This year (2026/27) Agenda for Change staff in England received 3.3% and doctors a headline 3.5%. 2026/27 awards explained by DHSC →

How it has been received

The Royal College of Nursing called the plan “a betrayal of nursing staff” and says it is likely to be below inflation. It says it has withdrawn from the pay review body process along with 13 of the 14 health unions, and wants ministers to negotiate directly.

Read the RCN’s response →

What happens next

2.4% is the government’s starting position, not a final award. The review bodies will weigh all the evidence and report in 2027, and the government then decides. For comparison, this year’s Agenda for Change award was confirmed in February.

What you can do now: check your current band and pay point with your HR or payroll team, talk to your union or professional body if you have questions, and look out for the review bodies’ reports in 2027.

If you work outside England

Scotland: Agenda for Change staff are on a two-year deal of 4.25% in 2025/26 and 3.75% in 2026/27, running to March 2027. Pay after that will be agreed in Scotland, through talks between the Scottish Government and NHS unions. The deal on gov.scot →

Wales and Northern Ireland: pay is decided by the Welsh Government and the Northern Ireland Executive.

Doctors anywhere in the UK: the same doctors’ review body makes recommendations, and each government decides whether to accept them for its own nation. About the review body →

Not employed by the NHS directly? If you work for a GP practice, community pharmacy or optical practice, your employer usually decides whether to pass on any NHS rise. Pay in social care and universities is set separately and is not tied to the NHS award.

IHPN-UK does not take sides on pay. This summary is to help you stay informed. For advice about your own pay, speak to your trade union, professional body or employer.
