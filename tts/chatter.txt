Running on empty? Here are three signs of burnout, and what can help.
Burnout isn't weakness. The World Health Organization describes it as the result of chronic workplace stress that hasn't been successfully managed.
The three signs are exhaustion, feeling distant or negative about your job, and feeling less effective at work.
Small steps help. Connect with people. Work smarter, not harder. And make time for yourself. If it lasts, talk to your GP or occupational health.

Know your rest rights at work. These are the legal minimums for adult workers, so save this.
If you work more than six hours a day, you have the right to one uninterrupted twenty-minute break. By law, it doesn't have to be paid. Your contract decides.
Between working days, you get eleven hours' rest, plus twenty-four hours off each week, or forty-eight hours each fortnight. In round-the-clock services like hospitals, you may get compensatory rest instead.
Every year, you get at least five point six weeks of paid leave. That's twenty-eight days for a five-day week, and your NHS contract may give you more.
