Writing a winning NHS job application: a guide for every healthcare role

How NHS shortlisting really works, how to write supporting information that scores, values-based interviews, and insider tips for nurses, midwives, doctors, consultants, GPs, public health, pharmacy, support workers, psychologists, dietitians and optometrists.

The three things that matter most
  1. You are scored against the person specification. Every essential criterion you don’t clearly show can cost you the interview.
  2. Examples beat adjectives. “I led the handover for 12 beds” scores; “I am a good leader” doesn’t.
  3. It must sound like you. Generic, AI-sounding statements stand out to shortlisters.

How shortlisting works

Recruiting managers score your application against the criteria in the person specification. They are usually marking a grid, not reading a story. Make it easy for them.

Writing supporting information that scores

  1. Print the person specificationHighlight every essential criterion. These are your must-cover list.
  2. Use the criteria as headingsCopy their wording, in their order. Shortlisters can tick you off quickly.
  3. One real example eachWhat you did, with numbers where you can: patients, staff, results, time saved.
  4. Cover the desirablesWhen many people meet the essentials, desirable criteria often decide who gets an interview.
  5. Never leave a box blankIncluding “any other relevant information”. NHSScotland’s own advice: “Don’t leave this blank!”
  6. Mirror the job, not your CVRewrite for every post. One statement sent everywhere rarely scores well.

Using AI tools such as ChatGPT

Fine to do
  • Check your spelling and grammar
  • Get help structuring your own examples
  • Practise interview questions
Avoid
  • Letting AI write your statement for you
  • Copying generic phrases that could fit anyone

NHS Employers’ guidance (August 2025) says AI-written applications “can often be generic, impersonal”, and that employers may ask you to talk through your application if they suspect AI use. Some trusts ask you to declare it. Your own words and real examples are always your strongest asset.

Values and interviews

NHS recruitment is values-based in all three nations. Learn the values for the nation you’re applying in, and have a real example ready for each one. Don’t just list them: show them.

England: NHS Constitution
  • Working together for patients
  • Respect and dignity
  • Commitment to quality of care
  • Compassion
  • Improving lives
  • Everyone counts
Scotland: NHSScotland
  • Care and compassion
  • Dignity and respect
  • Openness, honesty and responsibility
  • Quality and teamwork
Wales: NHS Wales
  • Putting quality and safety above all else
  • Eliminating harm, variation and waste
  • Prevention, health improvement and tackling inequality
  • Working in true partnership
  • Investing in our staff

Answer with STAR

  1. SSituation Set the scene in one line.
  2. TTask What you needed to do.
  3. AAction What you did. Say “I”, not “we”. This is the bit that scores.
  4. RResult What changed, and what you learned.

Questions worth asking at the end: “What does development look like for someone in this role in their first year?” and “What would success look like at six months?” They show you’re thinking ahead, and the answers tell you a lot about the team.

Tips for your role

Facts below come from official or professional bodies (see sources). Lines marked Our tip are practical advice from experience.

Nurses
  • RCN members can book a one-to-one telephone career coaching session covering CVs, applications, supporting statements and interviews.
  • Our tip Going for Band 6? Map examples of supervising students, running a shift or improving practice to each criterion.
  • Our tip Newly qualified? Ask at interview what preceptorship the team offers.
Midwives
  • The RCM’s early career midwives hub has a preceptorship guide, i-learn modules and webinars, useful for interview preparation.
  • Our tip Have one example ready on escalating a concern and one on supporting a family’s choices. Both map to the values above.
Doctors in training
  • Specialty training applications go through Oriel. About 60% arrive in the last 48 hours, and late applications are never accepted, so submit early.
  • You need 3 referees who supervised your clinical training in the last 2 years. Ask them before the window opens.
  • From 2027 you can make a maximum of five applications per recruitment round.
Consultants
  • NHS consultant interviews in England and Wales use an Advisory Appointments Committee: a legally constituted panel of five, including a lay member, the chief executive, the medical director, a consultant in the specialty and a Royal College assessor. Foundation trusts are advised to follow the same rules.
  • Our tip Ask the trust for the panel names and the day’s format in advance, and arrange to meet key colleagues before the interview.
GPs
  • To work as a GP in England you must join the Performers List, applying through PCSE Online with your GMC number.
  • Our tip Start the Performers List application as soon as you have an offer, and ask practices about protected time, list size and sessions.
Public health
  • Specialty training recruitment for 2027 is run nationally for England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, through Oriel. You submit one application only, and the selection centre is virtual. Applications opened on 22 October 2026.
  • Our tip For consultant posts, show population-level outcomes, not just projects you took part in.
Pharmacy
  • Our tip Put your GPhC number and any prescribing qualification at the top, with your area of practice.
  • Our tip Use numbers: medicines reviews done, errors reduced, clinics run.
Healthcare assistants and support workers
  • Completed the Care Certificate? Say so, with the year, and bring your evidence. Its standards are used to shape induction for people new to care.
  • Our tip Moving into a new area? Show transferable skills such as observations or dementia care, with a real example.
Psychologists
  • Practitioner psychologists are regulated by the HCPC: put your registration number on your application.
  • Applications for the clinical psychology doctorate (DClinPsy) go through the Clearing House for Postgraduate Courses in Clinical Psychology, not NHS Jobs.
Dietitians
  • The BDA has guidance on AI in recruitment. Using it to structure ideas or practise mock interviews is acceptable, but using it to misrepresent your skills or answer interview questions is not.
  • Our tip Bring one case that shows clinical reasoning and one that shows behaviour change.
Optometrists
  • Many optometry jobs are in community practice. The College of Optometrists jobs board lists roles from multiples and independents, all posted by College members. Hospital eye service posts appear on the NHS job sites above.
  • Our tip State your GOC number and any extra qualifications (for example independent prescribing) in the first lines.
Senior and Band 8a+ posts
  • Our tip Senior interviews often add a presentation, a stakeholder panel or a group exercise. Ask for the format when you’re invited, and practise the presentation to time.
  • Our tip Show impact at scale: budgets, teams, outcomes and how you led change across services.

References and the guaranteed interview scheme

ReferencesNHSScotland asks for at least two references, including your current or most recent employer, and never family or friends. Tell your referees before you apply.
Guaranteed interview schemeMany NHS employers run a guaranteed interview scheme: check the advert. If you have a disability under the Equality Act 2010, meet the essential criteria and opt in on the form, you are guaranteed an interview (not the job).

Before you press submit

  • Every essential criterion has a heading and an example
  • Desirable criteria covered where you can
  • No blank boxes, including “other information”
  • Registration number (NMC, GMC, GPhC, HCPC or GOC) included
  • Referees told and dates checked
  • Read aloud once: does it sound like you?
