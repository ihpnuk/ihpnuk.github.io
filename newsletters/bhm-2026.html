Leadership note

A message from our Founder & President

Black History Month is more than remembrance. It is a chance to recognise what becomes possible when talent, resilience and service are given room to flourish. This special edition celebrates five healthcare figures of Igbo heritage whose work has shaped medicine, research and public health far beyond their own careers.

IHPN-UK was founded on 28 August 2026, and this is our very first newsletter. Their stories remind us that progress is built across professions and generations. At IHPN-UK, we believe heritage should lead to action: mentoring the next generation, sharing knowledge, opening doors, building partnerships and helping healthcare professionals grow into the leaders their patients, organisations and communities need.

When I read these stories, I see people who look like us, who once stood where our newly qualified colleagues stand today. The best way to honour them is to lift someone else as we climb. As you read, I invite you to see both responsibility and possibility, and to turn inspiration into purposeful action.

With pride in our heritage and confidence in our future,
Ebele Nwosu
Founder & President, IHPN-UK

Featured leaders

Stories of impact

CTELandmark 2005 research on brain damage from repeated head impacts
Brain health

Dr Bennet Omalu

Physician, forensic pathologist and researcher

The doctor who changed the conversation on brain injury

While working as a medical examiner in the United States, Nigerian-born Dr Omalu identified abnormal brain changes in former professional American football players and published landmark research in 2005 on chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE).

He pursued questions that challenged established thinking, and brought the long-term effects of repeated head impacts into clinical, sporting and public debate.

Lesson for us: Follow the evidence, even when it is unpopular.

Source: Omalu et al., Neurosurgery, 2005

FU
1974Nigeria’s first open-heart operation
Cardiac surgery

Professor Fabian Udekwu

Cardiothoracic surgeon, UNTH Enugu

Pioneer of open-heart surgery in Nigeria

At the University of Nigeria Teaching Hospital (UNTH), Enugu, Professor Udekwu led the Nigerian surgical team that, with international colleagues, performed the country’s first open-heart operation.

His lasting legacy is institution-building: developing surgical capacity, supporting specialist teams and creating a pathway for advanced cardiothoracic practice in Nigeria.

Lesson for us: Breakthroughs last when you build the team around them.

Source: Eze & Ezemba, Texas Heart Institute Journal, 2007

UA
2012Prince Mahidol Award in Public Health
Public health

Professor Uche Amazigo

Former Director, WHO African Programme for Onchocerciasis Control

Transforming public health through community action

Professor Amazigo’s research revealed the social cost of river blindness, especially for women and girls. As Director of the WHO’s African Programme for Onchocerciasis Control, she championed community-directed treatment, putting local people at the heart of delivering ivermectin.

Lesson for us: Treat communities as partners, not passive recipients.

Source: Profile: Uche Amazigo, PubMed Central, 2022

IU
NanoNanoparticles that deliver medicines more effectively
Pharmaceutical science

Professor Dame Ijeoma Uchegbu

Professor of Pharmaceutical Nanoscience, UCL

Innovating drug delivery and widening access to science

At UCL, Professor Uchegbu uses nanoparticles to improve how medicines are delivered, and has taken her research towards real-world therapies through Nanomerics. Having overcome barriers in education and finance herself, she champions representation and inclusion in science.

Lesson for us: Open the door behind you for the next generation.

Source: Royal Society Fellow profile

OO
COVID-19Led major vaccine and treatment trials
Infectious disease

Professor Onyema Ogbuagu

Professor of Medicine (Infectious Diseases) and of Pharmacology, Yale School of Medicine

Advancing infectious-disease research, vaccines and global capacity

Professor Ogbuagu directs the Yale Antivirals and Vaccines Research Program, spanning HIV trials, antiviral development and research capacity-building in resource-limited settings. During the pandemic, he led major vaccine and treatment studies while helping the public understand fast-changing evidence.

Lesson for us: Lead across care, research and teaching, and mentor as you go.

Source: Yale School of Medicine profile

Who we are

A young network with deep roots

The Igbo Healthcare Professionals Network (UK) connects healthcare professionals of Igbo heritage across the UK. We support professional growth, strengthen representation and collaboration, develop future leaders, promote research and knowledge exchange, and build healthcare partnerships between the United Kingdom and Nigeria.
