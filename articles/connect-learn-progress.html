How Igbo healthcare professionals in the UK can connect, learn and progress

Five practical steps for building your network, growing your skills and taking your next career step, from the Igbo Healthcare Professionals Network (IHPN-UK).

By the IHPN-UK team • 4 November 2026 • 5-minute read

If you are an Igbo doctor, nurse, midwife, pharmacist or other healthcare professional in the UK, your experience is valuable, and it is too often overlooked. Some Igbo healthcare professionals in the UK trained in Nigeria or elsewhere abroad, others trained here, and many have rebuilt their careers in a new system. Yet it can still feel lonely when you are the only Igbo voice on your ward, team or practice.

This guide shares simple, practical ways to connect with others, keep learning and progress, and explains how the Igbo Healthcare Professionals Network (IHPN-UK) can help.

Behind every one of these numbers is a colleague with experience worth sharing. The more we connect, the more that experience can help the next person.

A sense of belonging

At our first members’ meeting in September 2026, members said that, above all, IHPN-UK is about a sense of belonging. Here is what they told us they want from it.

  • Real networkingA productive, engaged community where people work together, not a group that exists just for greetings.
  • Mentoring and career guidanceLearning from colleagues who have already walked the path, because progression is not about qualifications and experience alone.
  • WellbeingSupport with the pressures of healthcare work, including mental wellbeing.
  • Supporting our communitySharing the information and guidance that people can find hard to get.
  • Giving backUsing our skills and experience to support healthcare development in Nigeria.

The vision from that meeting is simple: those who have gained experience help others to rise, and those who are supported go on to support the next generation.

Five steps to connect and progress

  1. Find your people

    A network of colleagues who share your background makes it easier to ask the questions you might not ask at work, from settling into a new role to understanding how things work in the UK. Look for networks in your workplace, your professional body and your community, and join IHPN-UK.

  2. Ask for a mentor, and offer to be one

    A mentor does not need to be senior to you in every way. They just need to have walked part of the road you are on. If you are experienced, remember how much a single conversation helped you early on.

    When you apply to join, choose “Mentoring (as a mentee)” or “Mentoring (as a mentor)”. Our mentoring programme is planned, and we will contact you if and when it launches.

  3. Keep a record of your learning

    Nurses, midwives and nursing associates revalidate with the NMC every three years, and doctors revalidate with the GMC, usually every five years. Notes on training, reflections and feedback, kept as you go, make this much easier. Read the NMC revalidation guidance (opens in a new tab) and the GMC revalidation guidance (opens in a new tab).

  4. Plan your next step out loud

    Use your appraisal or one-to-ones to say where you want to be in two or three years, and ask what experience would get you there. People cannot back an ambition they do not know about.

  5. Share your story

    Your journey can open doors for someone else. We would love to feature members’ stories in future newsletters, always with their written consent. Email us yours at ihpnuk@outlook.com.

    Please do not include patient details or name your employer. We use your details only to discuss your story with you, and we publish nothing without your written consent. See our privacy notice.

How IHPN-UK can help

Who can join

Full membership is open to healthcare professionals who live and work in the UK and have at least one parent of Igbo ethnicity. Every working career stage is welcome, from newly qualified staff to senior leaders. Retired professionals may be invited to become Honorary Members. We are not accepting healthcare students at present. Read our full membership eligibility criteria.

Common questions

Does it cost anything to join?

There are currently no membership fees, and applying online takes about three minutes.

I trained in Nigeria. Can I join?

Yes, if you now live and work in the UK in healthcare and have at least one parent of Igbo ethnicity. Where you trained does not matter.

Can I mentor or be mentored?

Mentoring is planned but not yet running. When you apply, choose “Mentoring (as a mentee)” or “Mentoring (as a mentor)”, and we will contact you if and when the programme launches.
